# Atlas / JLST Corporate Defense Standard

Status: P0 governance control

Purpose: protect corporate control, Atlas intellectual property, production authority, evidence, and continuity against unauthorized transfer, dilution, seizure, compromise, or false ownership claims.

This document is an internal governance standard, not legal advice. Corporate/IP counsel must convert applicable controls into executed legal instruments.

## 1. Ownership and control

- Maintain a current authoritative ownership/capitalization record.
- No equity issuance, dilution, voting-right change, conversion right, control right, security interest in core Atlas IP, merger/change-of-control commitment, or IP transfer becomes effective without documented authorized approval and legal review.
- Company governing documents must define manager/member authority, reserved matters, transfer restrictions, admission/removal procedures, and approval thresholds.
- Economic participation does not automatically grant production, repository, domain, credential, recovery-root, or policy-administration authority.

## 2. Atlas IP chain of title

- Maintain an inventory of copyrightable software, documentation, designs, datasets where protectable, trademarks/brands, trade secrets, domains, and contractual IP rights.
- Every employee, contractor, developer, consultant, or vendor contributing protectable work must have appropriate confidentiality and IP-assignment terms before privileged contribution.
- Preserve source history, authorship/contribution evidence, signed assignments, release evidence, and material third-party license records.
- No contributor receives unilateral authority to transfer or encumber Atlas IP.

## 3. Trademark and copyright program

- Perform professional trademark clearance before relying on federal registration for ATLAS or related marks.
- Maintain a trademark docket for applications, office actions, renewals, specimens, jurisdictions, and deadlines.
- Evaluate qualifying Atlas computer programs and documentation for U.S. Copyright Office registration with counsel, including trade-secret-safe deposit strategy where appropriate.
- Registration status must never be represented publicly as granted until official evidence exists.

## 4. Trade-secret protection

Confidential by default: nonpublic architecture, security controls, proprietary prompts/policies, scoring logic, private datasets, credentials, counterparty intelligence, unreleased code, internal threat models, and confidential commercial methods.

Controls:
- need-to-know access;
- confidentiality agreements;
- repository/environment separation;
- secrets never committed to source;
- access logging;
- immediate offboarding/revocation;
- approved disclosure process;
- evidence of reasonable secrecy measures.

## 5. Infrastructure control separation

Critical systems include source control, deployment, domains/DNS, corporate email/identity, cloud, banking/payment administration, signing keys, recovery systems, and evidence vaults.

Requirements:
- phishing-resistant MFA for privileged accounts where supported;
- no shared administrator credentials;
- least privilege;
- separate recovery mechanisms;
- audit logging;
- protected backup/recovery identities;
- no single external developer/vendor/partner controls all critical systems;
- consequential ownership, recovery-root, domain, or production-authority changes require independent approval.

## 6. Financing defense

Before signing financing/investment documents, legal review is required for:
- equity and dilution;
- board/manager rights;
- voting/veto rights;
- preferred rights;
- conversion rights;
- warrants/options;
- security interests/liens;
- IP covenants;
- negative covenants;
- change-of-control provisions;
- drag/tag rights;
- information rights;
- default remedies;
- personal guarantees.

Atlas operational credentials are never consideration for investment.

## 7. Corporate evidence vault

Preserve tamper-resistant copies of:
- formation and governing documents;
- ownership/capitalization records;
- tax/entity records;
- resolutions/consents;
- IP assignments and contributor agreements;
- NDAs/confidentiality agreements;
- trademark/copyright filings and official correspondence;
- material contracts and financing documents;
- domain ownership evidence;
- source/release provenance;
- security and access-change evidence;
- backup/recovery attestations.

Material records require retention, integrity verification, access control, and recoverable backups.

## 8. Atlas enforcement invariant

Atlas must not execute or assist a consequential corporate-control action merely because a requester has technical access.

For protected actions, require:

principal identity -> current corporate authority -> exact proposed action -> independent approval where required -> legal/policy evidence -> execution -> immutable evidence

Protected actions include ownership changes, equity issuance, IP assignment, production/recovery-root transfer, credential-root replacement, domain transfer, policy-root modification, and destructive evidence-vault operations.

Missing, stale, conflicting, or unverifiable authority => FAIL CLOSED.

## 9. Required legal workstream

Obtain qualified counsel review for:
1. governing/operating agreement and control provisions;
2. capitalization and ownership records;
3. employee/contractor confidentiality and invention/IP assignment package;
4. trademark clearance and filing strategy;
5. copyright registration strategy;
6. trade-secret program;
7. customer/vendor/partner terms affecting IP;
8. financing document review protocol;
9. insurance review (including appropriate cyber/E&O coverage);
10. dispute, succession, incapacity, and business-continuity planning.

## 10. Evidence rule

A control is not considered complete because it appears in this document. Completion requires executed legal documents, verified technical configuration, official filing/registration evidence where applicable, and periodic revalidation.
